# English quality baseline: today's stack and pristine AOSP (2026-11-15)

Stage 0 of [the plan for SebType's own Android keyboard](../plans/android-own-keyboard.md) (#231): the English
harness `android/test/english/run.sh` and the numbers every later stage is held to (§2's quality bar). It compares
today's English, FUTO's decoder with its transformer `ml4_q6_k.gguf`, with the pristine AOSP LatinIME decoder that
the new keyboard is built on. Both run as host builds on yanglab16 and are driven key by key through their own JNI
functions, with no emulator, as #46 did.

**Summary.** Today's stack fixes 80.7 % of phone typos against AOSP's 71.1 %, and changes 0.3 % of correct words,
the same as AOSP. Only today's stack predicts next words: 37.8 % of chat and 32.1 % of news next words are in its
top 3. AOSP's decoder predicts none, because neither AOSP's English dictionary nor SebType's has word pairs. On
Wikipedia's misspellings, which come with no sentence around them, today's stack fixes only 37.5 % against
AOSP's 69.7 % ([below](#misspellings-without-context)). Today's stack takes 8.5 ms per key at p50 and 24 ms at p95.
AOSP's decoder takes 1.1 / 1.9 ms. Two runs gave the same quality numbers, and their latencies differ by under 3 %.

## Misspellings without context

The Wikipedia misspellings are single words with nothing before them. Today's transformer then sees the start of
a text, and of the 937 it doesn't fix, it leaves 513 as typed, turns 243 into the typed word with a capital
("correspondant" → "Correspondant"), commits another word for 158, and capitalises the right word for 23. In the
app, auto-capitalisation would have typed the first letter of such a word as a capital, so part of the 243 is the
set's doing. The 513 left as typed are not: with no context the model seldom gives the "autocorrect" verdict, and
FUTO's merge then keeps the typed word. #46 measured FUTO's decoder alone and expected the transformer "only
add[s]" to it. That holds mid-sentence (phone typos, +9.6 points) but not for a word on its own. The bar in §2
compares later stacks with this column on the same set, so the gap does not need fixing for stage 0. It shows
where a model trained with the #46 error mix (stage 7) can gain.

## What is measured

- **Typo fixed:** the word typed with the set's touch points and a space after it commits the intended word, by
  each stack's own autocorrect rule at the "modest" threshold (0.185).
- **Correct word wrongly changed:** the same for words typed without a typo. A space commits a different word.
- **Next word in the top 3:** with the sentence so far as context and nothing typed, the next word is among the
  first three suggestions, ignoring case.
- **Latency:** each word is typed one key at a time. Every key is one call that decodes the whole prefix, as the
  app does on each key, and the time of that call is one per-key sample. A word's time is its keys' sum. The model
  runs on one thread, as on the phone. These times are yanglab16's (AMD EPYC 9224), not a phone's. They compare
  the stacks with each other and with later runs of the harness, not with Decisions #2's phone budgets.

Today's stack restates FUTO's Kotlin around its native code: `LanguageModel.getSuggestions` (the context
trimming, the safeguard and the model's verdict) and `LanguageModelFacilitator.processAndMergeSuggestions` (the
3.4 weight, the both-agree rule and the 14-word list). The AOSP stack follows AOSP's
`Suggest.getSuggestedWordsForNonBatchInput`. Neither stack has learned words, a user dictionary or contacts.

## Sets

All from openly licensed text, none of it the user's words or data (`android/test/english/sets/LICENSE`).
`make_sets.py` makes them again identically: seed 231, and Wikinews articles cached by revision.

| Set | Items | From |
|---|---|---|
| phone-typos | 1,000 words, length ≥ 3 | Sentences from oasst1 chat prompts (Apache-2.0) and English Wikinews (CC BY 2.5), typed with Gaussian touch noise (σ 30 px on 108 × 160 px keys) and #46's phone error mix (swap 6 %, then substitution, omission and insertion 5 : 3 : 1; two errors in 10 % of words) |
| clean | 1,000 words, length ≥ 2 | The same sentences, typed with touch noise and no errors |
| misspellings | 1,500 pairs | Wikipedia's "Lists of common misspellings/For machines" (CC BY-SA 4.0), as in #46, with no context |
| next-chat | 2,701 next words | 250 oasst1 prompts |
| next-news | 1,927 next words | 150 Wikinews articles |

The keyboard is a 1080 × 640 px QWERTY layout with AOSP's proximity grid and its "holo" touch correction.

## Not measured

- Keystroke savings (§2: reported, not gated) is left to stage 7, when it compares two models.
- Gesture typing, learned words and the bigrams of a user's history.

## Running it

`android/test/english/run.sh` builds both decoders on yanglab16 (in its build lock, on at most half its cores),
runs every stack on every set twice, and checks that the runs agree. Quality must be equal and per-key p50
within 25 %. It writes this table to `~/scratch/SebType/<issue>/english/table.md`. Both runs take about 15 minutes together.
AOSP LatinIME is fetched at commit `127336e9f29d` into `~/scratch/SebType/cache/`. FUTO's decoder and model are
compiled and read where they are in `android/app/src/main/`.
